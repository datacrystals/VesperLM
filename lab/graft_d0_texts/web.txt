The city council approved the new riverside park plan on Tuesday after two
years of public consultation. Construction begins in March, with the first
walking paths opening to residents by late summer. The design keeps the
existing plane trees and adds a small amphitheater near the old boathouse.

Local bakery Golden Crust won the regional sourdough award for the third year
running. The owner credits a long cold ferment and a starter maintained since
2011. Weekend queues now start before seven in the morning; the shop added a
second oven to keep up with demand.

Travelers heading to the coast this holiday should expect delays on the
highway between junctions 12 and 17, where resurfacing work continues through
the month. The alternative inland route adds about twenty minutes but passes
several farm shops worth a stop, especially for stone fruit in season.

The community library will extend opening hours on Thursdays after a volunteer
drive recruited twelve new helpers. A repair cafe launches the first Saturday
of each month, bringing together people who can fix bicycles, small
appliances, clothing, and furniture rather than sending them to landfill.

Housing statistics for the quarter show asking rents flat for the second
period in a row, while the number of listings rose by nearly a fifth. Analysts
attribute the shift to new apartments completing near the transit hub and to
remote workers relocating from larger cities further inland.

The annual street fair returns with more than two hundred stalls, a stage for
local bands, and the usual tug-of-war between the fire station and the rowing
club. Organizers ask attendees to bring reusable cups; last year the fair
diverted most of its waste from landfill through a volunteer sorting line.

A new night bus route links the hospital, the station, and the university
quarter every thirty minutes until two in the morning. Transport officials
say the route will be reviewed after six months based on ridership, with the
possibility of weekend extensions if evening demand proves steady.

Farmers report an early but modest apple crop after a dry spring and a cool
June. Prices at the market are expected to stay close to last year's, with
heritage varieties particularly plentiful. The cooperative is again running
its juice-pressing days, where anyone can bring windfalls for pasteurized juice.

The old cinema on Mill Street reopens this weekend after a careful restoration
that preserved its 1930s facade and interior plasterwork. The program mixes
new releases with classic double bills, and the balcony has been reopened for
the first time in decades. Tickets for the opening gala sold out within hours.

Neighborhood associations are testing quieter waste collection times after
complaints about early-morning noise. Under the trial, glass and paper will be
collected after seven rather than at half past five. Residents can comment
through the city website; a decision on making the change permanent is due in
the new year.
