The quadratic formula states that for ax^2 + bx + c = 0 with a nonzero, the
solutions are x = (-b ± sqrt(b^2 - 4ac)) / (2a). The discriminant b^2 - 4ac
determins the nature of the roots: positive gives two distinct real roots,
zero a repeated root, and negative a pair of complex conjugates.

A geometric series 1 + r + r^2 + ... + r^n converges to 1/(1-r) when |r| < 1
as n goes to infinity. The partial sum of the first n terms is (1 - r^n)/(1 - r),
which is the identity used to convert repeating decimals into fractions.

Integration by parts is the product rule run backwards: the integral of u dv
equals uv minus the integral of v du. Choosing u to be something that
simplifies when differentiated — a logarithm or a polynomial — and dv to be
something easy to integrate is the standard strategy.

The prime number theorem describes the asymptotic distribution of primes: the
count of primes below x is approximately x / ln(x). Equivalently, the gap
between consecutive primes near x grows like ln(x). Probabilistic heuristics
suggest the twin prime conjecture is true, but a proof remains open.

Bayes' theorem updates a belief after evidence: the posterior probability of a
hypothesis given data equals the likelihood times the prior, divided by the
marginal probability of the data. In practice the denominator is often the
hard part, which is why sampling methods and variational approximations exist.

A matrix is diagonalizable when it has a full set of eigenvectors. Symmetric
real matrices are always diagonalizable with orthogonal eigenvectors, which is
why covariance matrices decompose cleanly into principal components: the
eigenvectors give directions of variance and the eigenvalues its magnitude.

To prove a statement by induction, verify the base case, assume it holds for n,
and derive it for n+1. Strong induction assumes all cases up to n instead of
just n itself; the two forms are logically equivalent but one or the other
often makes the inductive step fall out directly.

The Pythagorean theorem says a^2 + b^2 = c^2 for a right triangle with legs a
and b and hypotenuse c. Its converse also holds: any triangle whose sides
satisfy the relation is right-angled. The distance formula in the plane is the
theorem applied to the horizontal and vertical differences of two points.

Convergence of sequences: a sequence converges to L when for every epsilon > 0
there is an index beyond which all terms lie within epsilon of L. A sequence
that is bounded and monotone converges; this is the monotone convergence
theorem and it is the workhorse behind many existence proofs.

0.999... equals 1. One quick argument: let x = 0.999..., then 10x = 9.999...,
so 10x - x = 9, giving x = 1. The more careful argument is that the sequence
of partial sums 0.9, 0.99, 0.999 is Cauchy and its limit is the real number 1
by the completeness of the reals.
